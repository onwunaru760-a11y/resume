About Me

I'm a penultimate-year medical student who got pulled toward a different question than most of my classmates: not just whether a treatment is right, but whether the AI recommending it can be trusted — and whether it knows when it isn't sure. Most clinical AI gets graded once, on the easy cases, and shipped. I got interested in the harder edge: the wrong dose that looks plausible, the lab value that means something different depending on who's holding it. So I build small, working tools that test clinical AI the way I'd work up a real patient — looking for what doesn't fit, and staying honest about what's still uncertain. Most recently I ran the literature review behind the EU MDR update of an AI breast cancer screening device, which meant giving published studies the same suspicion I give AI outputs.

Findings — Work & Projects

Thermalytix Literature Review for the EU MDR Clinical Evaluation

Niramai Health Analytix · Systematic Review · PRISMA · Diagnostic Accuracy Appraisal
Chief complaint
An AI breast cancer screening device needed its clinical evidence updated from the old EU MDD to the EU MDR standard, with a search another reviewer could rerun and a reason on file for every study left out.
Workup
Searched PubMed, the Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov and Europe PMC for 2020 to 2026. Removed duplicates, screened 248 records, assessed 23 reports at full text against the existing exclusion criteria, added three codes where they fell short, and extracted and scored each included study on a 100 point appraisal grid. Re-checked my own screening with a random sample and a full read of the borderline papers.
Finding
513 database records became 5 eligible studies. Most of the thermography AI literature was tested on one public image database, not on prospectively recruited patients, and some of the clinical studies reported accuracy only in women who already had a suspicious lesion.

Clinical Note Eval Generator

Python · Rule-Based Error Injection · Eval Dataset Design
Chief complaint
Clinical AI tools rarely get tested against errors that actually matter — a wrong dose, a missed allergy conflict — because building a labeled test set by hand doesn't scale.
Workup
Built a generator that produces realistic clinical notes across four scenarios, deliberately injecting one of six labeled error types (dosage, allergy conflict, contradictory vitals, drug interaction, unit error, dropped follow-up) at controlled difficulty — mixed with clean notes to catch false positives too.
Finding
A repeatable, growing test set for scoring whether a clinical AI system catches what a clinician would — and stays quiet when nothing's wrong.

Lab-Value-to-Plain-English Explainer

Python · Rule-Based Clinical Reasoning · Confidence Scoring
Chief complaint
Most lab-explainer tools confidently interpret every number, even the ones where "normal" depends on context the tool was never given.
Workup
Built an interpreter across five common lab tests that separates its confidence level from its verdict, and explicitly names what's missing — sex, build, comorbidities — whenever a result is genuinely ambiguous.
Finding
A tool that says "I'm not sure, and here's what would help" instead of guessing with false confidence.
